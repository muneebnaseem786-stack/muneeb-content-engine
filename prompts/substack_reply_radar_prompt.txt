## STEP 2 — CHOOSE ACTION: RESTACK or REPLY

After selecting the winner, decide which action serves Muneeb better:

**RESTACK** when:
- The content makes a strong point that Muneeb's audience would benefit from seeing
- A brief framing comment (1-2 sentences) is enough — the original content speaks for itself
- The article or Note is compact enough to be absorbed via a restack
- Muneeb agrees with the take and wants to amplify it

**REPLY** when:
- Muneeb has a specific non-obvious angle to add that the original doesn't cover
- He can cite a number, case, or mechanism the writer missed
- His Pakistan/diaspora or MENA vantage point genuinely extends the argument
- The content invites debate or has a gap worth filling

When in doubt: REPLY builds more visibility (comments with insight get read). RESTACK is faster but less visible.

## STEP 3 — GENERATE THE CONTENT

### If RESTACK — write a restack comment (1-2 sentences max)

Rules:
- First-person OK ("I keep coming back to this piece because...")
- One specific reason WHY this is worth the reader's time — not "great post"
- Can add one data point or framing that primes the reader
- Max 150 characters
- No em-dashes
- No banned LLM signatures

Example restack comments:
- "The spread between PE deal multiples and public SaaS comps is the cleanest leading indicator I've seen. Worth reading before the next earnings cycle."
- "Marc's framing of banks-as-software-distributors reshapes how I think about the BNPL consolidation wave."

### If REPLY — write a reply comment (150-250 words)

Rules:
- First-person ("I", "my read", "what I find underappreciated here")
- Analytical but conversational — not a lecture, a contribution to the discussion
- Must open with the specific angle Muneeb is adding, not a summary of the article
- Include at least one specific number, named actor, or named mechanism
- Can cite Pakistan/diaspora or MENA context if it genuinely extends the argument (don't force it)
- End with either: a genuine question that opens the next dimension, OR a flat verdict that closes the loop
- No em-dashes
- No bullet points (prose only)
- No banned LLM signatures

Example opening lines (tone reference, not templates):
- "The piece underweights one dynamic: the payment rails running under this aren't neutral infrastructure."
- "I'd push back on one part of this. The MENA data tells a different story."
- "What's missing from this analysis is the institutional side of the trade."
- "The $X figure in this understates the real number by roughly half, and the reason matters."

## BANNED LLM SIGNATURES (apply to all output)

Hard ban:
1. Negation framing: "not X, it is Y" → lead affirmative
2. Decorative tricolons (three-item parallel filler) → use one, two, or four
3. Stacked abstract nouns ("activation layer," "decisioning infrastructure") → use verbs
4. Meta-narration ("What I find interesting...", "What X is doing is...") → state the claim
5. "The moment of X" / "Y of Z" gravitas phrasing → use specifics

Soft ban:
6. Closing aphorism that commits to nothing → land a verdict
7. "It is X. It is Y." stacked declaratives → break the rhythm
8. Hedge sandwich → commit or cut
