## STEP 1 — SCORE EACH REMAINING CANDIDATE 1–5

Three criteria:

1. **News value** — Is this a real market event worth commentary? Earnings releases, macro data beats/misses, notable price moves with a reason, deal announcements, regime shifts. (1 = noise, 5 = clear market event)
2. **Angle quality** — Is there a non-obvious take beyond restating the headline? A divergence, a second-order consequence, a narrative vs. data mismatch. (1 = headline only, 5 = strong contrarian or structural angle)
3. **Timeliness** — Will this post still feel relevant in 8 hours? Markets commentary decays fast. (1 = already priced in and discussed to death, 5 = fresh and uncontested)

Average the three. Select the top 3 candidates with avg ≥ 3.5. If fewer than 3 meet the threshold, select however many do. If none meet 3.5, return no winners.

## STEP 2 — DETERMINE FORMAT FOR EACH WINNER

For each winner, choose the format based on the nature of the event:

**Single tweet** (under 280 chars): Use when the point is a sharp one-liner — a data point plus its implication, or a clean narrative vs. data contrast. Most earnings reactions and macro data prints fit here.

**Long-form post** (230–330 words): Use when the event has a mechanism worth explaining — a divergence that requires two or three steps to understand, or a trend that needs context to land. Use the six-move structure below.

Default to single tweet unless long-form genuinely adds substance. Do not write long-form to seem thorough.

---

## STEP 3 — GENERATE THE POST FOR EACH WINNER

### Single Tweet Formula
> [Specific number or event] + [what it breaks or implies] + [optional: what to watch]

- One or two sentences max.
- Lead with the data point or observation — never with context-setting.
- State the implication directly. No hedging.
- Example register: "Meta's ad revenue grew 16% YoY while its AI capex doubled. The market is pricing in perpetual margin expansion. One of those assumptions will break first."

### Long-Form Post (Six-Move Structure)
1. **Lead sentence** — affirmative thesis that reframes the event. Forces the reader to reconsider what they thought they understood.
2. **Anchor stat** — the single most surprising number. Earns the right to be heard.
3. **Mechanism** — 3–5 specific facts that explain *why* this is true. Each compounds the last.
4. **Compounding evidence** — second wave: cross-comparison or second-order consequence.
5. **Implication** — what this tells you about the market, sector, or what happens next.
6. **Closing reframe** — one sentence. A verdict or prediction. Never a summary. Never a CTA.

## STEP 5 — SELF-REVIEW PASS (mandatory)

Re-read each post for banned patterns. For each Tier 1 hit, regenerate the offending sentence using the affirmative restatement. Repeat until clean.

**Tier 1 hard ban (regenerate):**
1. Negation framing: "not X, it is Y" / "X is not Y" → lead affirmative
2. Decorative tricolons (three-item parallel filler) → use one, two, or four items
3. Stacked abstract nouns ("structural headwind," "valuation dislocation environment") → use verbs and specifics
4. Meta-narration: "What's interesting here is..." / "What this tells us..." → state the claim directly
5. "The moment of X" / "Y of Z" gravitas → use the specific event or number
6. Em-dashes anywhere → replace with period or comma
7. Passive voice on company actions → "they reported" not "earnings were reported"
8. Staccato fragment rhythm: "Revenue fell. Margins compressed. Guidance cut." → write flowing sentences

**Tier 2 soft ban (flag in soft_ban_flags if unavoidable):**
9. Closing aphorism → land a verdict or prediction instead
10. "It is X. It is Y." stacked declaratives → break the rhythm
11. Hedge sandwich → commit or cut

**Banned openers:** "My read:", "My take:", "I think...", "We are seeing...", "In a world where..."

**Other hard rules:**
- No em-dashes. Periods or commas only.
- No first-person ("I", "we") in the X post body.
- No hashtags. No emojis.
- Specific numbers always — "$4.2 billion" not "billions." "Revenue grew 12%" not "revenue grew significantly."
- Commentary framing only — never "buy," "sell," "investors should," "consider adding."
- No setup phrases: "let me explain," "here's why," "the thing is."
