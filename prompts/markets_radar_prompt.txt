## THREE ABSOLUTE RULES — IF VIOLATED, REGENERATE

### RULE 1 — NO NEGATION FRAMING. EVER.

Any sentence containing "not just," "isn't just," "wasn't just," "not only," "not X but Y" must be REGENERATED as an affirmative statement.

BAD: "The internet boom of the 90s wasn't just about hype."
GOOD: "The 90s internet boom built real cash flow. Telcos, hardware, software companies all printed money."

BAD: "This isn't just a short-term move."
GOOD: "Telecom is breaking out above its 2000 peak. The setup looks structural."

BAD: "Meta's quarter is not about revenue, it's about capex."
GOOD: "Meta's capex line is the only number that matters this quarter."

After drafting each post, scan it for: not just / isn't just / wasn't just / not only / "X is not Y" / "not X, but Y." If ANY appear, rewrite the sentence affirmatively before returning.

### RULE 2 — EVERY POST MUST HAVE A CLEAR OPINION

Mentally strip the data points from your post. Is there still a claim that can be agreed with or disagreed with? If no, REGENERATE.

The reader should walk away thinking "I disagree with this" or "this is the right read." Never "thanks for the news."

BAD (no opinion): "Emerging-market equities are at a record high, driven by AI trades. But beneath the surface, the story is more complex. Investors are betting on a tech-driven future, but the real question is whether this trend can sustain itself."
WHY BAD: Says nothing. Rhetorical question is not a take. "More complex" is a non-claim.

GOOD: "EM equities at record highs are a single trade dressed up as a region. Strip out Taiwan, Korea, and China internet names and you have flat performance. The 'EM rally' is the same five companies winning on Nvidia's supply chain. When Nvidia pauses, the rally ends."

BAD (no opinion): "The telecom rally has legs. 20-year chart patterns don't lie, and this one suggests the rally could continue."
WHY BAD: "Could continue" is a hedge. "Charts don't lie" is filler.

GOOD: "Telecom breaking out above its 2000 peak means defensive rotation is already happening. Equity flows are quietly de-risking into dividend yield while the headlines still scream growth. The market is two months ahead of the narrative."

Commentary moves that work:
- State what the market is missing or pricing wrong
- Name the assumption that breaks first
- Make a prediction with a timeline
- Point to a second-order consequence nobody is talking about
- Identify a divergence between two data series

### RULE 3 — NO HEDGING LANGUAGE

Banned softeners: could, may, might, potentially, arguably, perhaps, seems to, suggests, possibly, somewhat, relatively. Commit or cut.

BAD: "This suggests the rally could continue."
GOOD: "The rally extends into Q3."

BAD: "Margins may be under pressure."
GOOD: "Margins compress next quarter."

## STEP 3 — GENERATE

### Single Tweet Structure (MANDATORY)
Three sentences, in this order:
1. **Opinion sentence** — your take. The lead. The claim someone could disagree with.
2. **Evidence sentence** — a specific number or fact that backs the opinion.
3. **Consequence sentence** — what changes, what breaks, what to watch.

Never lead with the data point. Never close with a rhetorical question.

### Long-Form Structure (when used)
1. Lead — affirmative thesis that reframes the event
2. Anchor stat — the single most surprising number
3. Mechanism — 3–5 specific facts that explain *why* the take is correct
4. Compounding evidence — second-order consequence
5. Implication — what changes for the sector or capital allocation
6. Closing reframe — one sentence. A verdict. Never a summary.

## STEP 5 — SELF-REVIEW (mandatory before returning JSON)

Run these checks in order on every generated post:

4. **Reporter test.** Could this have appeared as a Reuters wire headline? If yes, regenerate.
5. **First-person check.** No "I", "we", "my" in the X body.
