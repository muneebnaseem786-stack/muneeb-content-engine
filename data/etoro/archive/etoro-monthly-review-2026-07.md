# eToro Monthly Review ,  July 2026

## Month's Market Data
- $SPX500: The S&P 500 closed July 2026 with a modest gain of approximately +0.08%, finishing at 7,489.67 points on July 31. The index saw an intra-month high of 7,581.50 on July 15 and an intra-month low of 7,316.15 on July 29.
- $NDX: The Nasdaq Composite experienced a significant decline in July, falling approximately -13.48% to close at 25,373.85 points on July 31. This marked its "worst month for chips since the financial crisis". The index hit an intra-month high of 29,771.89 on July 15 and an intra-month low of 24,442.94 on July 29.
- $BTC: Bitcoin ended July with a gain of approximately +5.39%, starting the month around $60,336.43 on July 1 and closing at $63,588.34 on July 31. It traded above $60,000 early in the month but experienced institutional outflows on July 24 and dipped to a three-week low around $62,702 on July 31.
- $ETH: Ethereum was a strong performer in July, gaining approximately +15.79% to close at $1,875.73 on July 31, a rebound from June's decline. AMBCrypto reported a 20.3% gain for Ethereum in July, its strongest monthly performance in a year. It started the month around $1,619.99 on July 1 and saw a significant institutional inflow of over $1 billion into ETFs on July 6.
- Other major moves: Oil prices saw significant volatility, with Brent crude surging to $102 per barrel by July 23 amid escalating Middle East tensions, re-igniting inflation concerns. The semiconductor sector faced a sharp mid-month sell-off due to concerns over AI infrastructure spending valuations, despite strong underlying demand for AI chips.

## Key Macro Events
- **June Jobs Report:** Released on July 2, showing a weaker-than-expected 57,000 new non-farm payrolls, shifting expectations towards potential Federal Reserve monetary easing.
- **US Market Holiday:** Independence Day was observed on July 3, resulting in a shortened trading week.
- **US CPI for June:** Released on July 14, the CPI data was received positively by risk assets, with Bitcoin seeing a bounce.
- **FOMC Meeting:** Held on July 28-29, where the Federal Reserve maintained its target range for the federal funds rate at 3.50% to 3.75%, noting that inflation remained above its 2% goal.
- **Geopolitical Tensions:** The Middle East saw ongoing US-Iran confrontation, with renewed sanctions and US strikes, impacting oil prices and creating market uncertainty throughout the month.

## Held Names That Reported / Moved Materially
- **$TSM (Taiwan Semiconductor):** Reported strong Q2 earnings on July 16, with revenue up 36% and net income up 77.4%, driven by high-performance computing demand. The company raised its 2026 capex to $60B-$64B and committed an additional $100B to US production, reinforcing its AI compute leadership. However, the stock saw a slight after-hours decline as investors considered higher capital spending and near-term margin pressure.
- **$NFLX (Netflix):** Reported Q2 earnings on July 15, beating EPS but missing revenue forecasts. Shares plunged 8.58% in after-hours trading. Netflix also began rolling out its new "Enhanced Interactive Storytelling" feature, leveraging AI for adaptive narratives.
- **$TSLA (Tesla):** Q2 earnings on July 22 showed record revenue and deliveries but missed profit estimates, with negative free cash flow due to aggressive spending on Robotaxi and Optimus. The stock plummeted 14.52%. Earlier in July, $TSLA reported strong Q2 deliveries, its first YoY growth in two years, and announced over $9B in Megapack orders.
- **$GOOG (Alphabet):** Q2 earnings on July 22 beat revenue and EPS, driven by an 82% surge in Google Cloud revenue. However, a significant increase in capital expenditure (doubling to $44.9B) and raised full-year guidance led to negative free cash flow and a 7.13% stock decline. Reports also indicated delays for its Gemini 3.5 Pro AI model.
- **$AMD (Advanced Micro Devices):** Received multiple analyst upgrades and positive sentiment for its MI300X AI accelerators and 2027 pipeline. Speculation grew about Anthropic becoming a major customer. $AMD launched its MI400 AI accelerator and entered a 15-year, $14B AI data center agreement with Core Scientific.
- **$AMZN (Amazon):** Announced a $25B bond sale to fund AI investments. AWS secured a $1.5B cloud/AI deal with a global enterprise. Reported strong Q2 earnings on July 30, with net sales up 20% and AWS sales growing 37% (its fastest in 18 quarters). Management raised 2026 capex to $220B, noting demand still exceeds capacity.
- **$HOOD (Robinhood):** Announced significant global expansion efforts, including the public mainnet launch of Robinhood Chain and new product offerings. Also launched "Trump Accounts," a new government-backed investment initiative. Q2 earnings on July 29 beat revenue and EPS, driven by transaction-based revenues, though crypto revenue declined.
- **$BABA (Alibaba):** Settled a $600M DOJ dispute and received a temporary reprieve from a US lobbying ban. Its Qianwen AI was integrated into Apple's AI features for Chinese users. However, the company faced an investigation for securities law violations related to alleged illicit AI access.
- **$IREN (Iris Energy):** Shares plummeted on July 2 due to concerns over an $800M founder equity compensation plan. Later in the month, it announced $2.8B in new AI cloud contracts, raising its year-end 2026 AI Cloud annualized run-rate revenue target to over $4B.
- **$COIN (Coinbase):** Secured UK regulatory authorization for derivatives and equities and received a license to offer crypto trading and custody to institutional clients in Asia.
- **$EOSE (Eos Energy):** Announced a rights offering to fund its Frontier Power USA joint venture. Preliminary Q2 results showed record revenue and backlog but anticipated gross margin loss due to manufacturing scale-up. The rights offering closed, raising $37.7M.
- **$PLTR (Palantir):** Reported strong Q4 2025 results (July 10) with robust 2026 revenue guidance, driven by AI demand in government and commercial sectors. Later, shares declined due to UK regulatory scrutiny on its NHS contract and the emergence of an open-source alternative called "World Monitor".
- **$NVDA (NVIDIA):** Delayed its Kyber rack-scale architecture and Rubin Ultra chips to 2028 due to manufacturing challenges. CEO Jensen Huang announced a $500B business partnership with South Korea's SK Group.
- **$GLXY (Galaxy Digital):** Secured a $75M, 15-year naming rights deal for Texas Tech University's football stadium, becoming its official digital assets partner. Launched an initiative with up to $5M in grants to safeguard Bitcoin against quantum computing threats. Priced a $3.507B senior secured notes offering to fund a major data center project.
- **$TAO (Bittensor):** Cryptocurrency exchange MEXC integrated with Bittensor validator Yuma, enabling staking for its 40M users.
- **$TE (T1 Energy):** Announced an $85M cost overrun and production delay for its G2_Austin solar-cell facility, pushing initial production to Q1 2027 and triggering a securities investigation.

## Cluster Behaviour
- **AI compute (semis + hyperscalers + AI software):** This cluster experienced a divergent July. While the $NDX, home to many AI names, suffered its "worst month for chips since the financial crisis" with a significant decline, our core AI compute holdings demonstrated resilience and continued strategic execution. $AMD, $TSM, and $AMZN all reported strong earnings or major new partnerships and investments that reinforced their long-term AI theses. There's a clear theme of massive capex spending across the industry, with demand still outstripping supply.
- **Crypto-related (coins + miners + exchanges + rails):** After a challenging June, this cluster showed a notable rebound in July. Both $BTC and $ETH posted positive monthly returns, with Ethereum leading the way. While there were periods of institutional outflows, particularly mid-month for Bitcoin, these were often followed by strong inflows, showing a more balanced institutional sentiment than previous months. Our crypto rails (Coinbase, Robinhood) continued to expand globally and diversify their offerings.
- **China re-rating ($BABA, $FUTU, $COPX):** $BABA showed mixed signals, navigating regulatory settlements and new AI partnerships with Apple, but also facing new investigations. $FUTU continued to face class-action lawsuits and regulatory penalties. This cluster remains sensitive to geopolitical and regulatory developments.
- **Big tech non-AI-primary ($NFLX, $TSLA, $SPOT):** This cluster had a challenging month, with $TSLA and $NFLX both seeing significant stock declines post-earnings. While operational metrics often showed strength (Tesla deliveries, Netflix ad-tier growth), market reaction was cautious to increased spending ($TSLA) or revenue misses ($NFLX).
- **Tail bets / thematic ($EOSE, $SOFI, $TE, $ONDS, $OPEN, $SIVE.ST):** This group saw both positive and negative developments. $EOSE secured new funding and reported record preliminary revenue, while $IREN announced major new AI cloud contracts. However, $TE faced significant project delays and a securities investigation, and $SIVE.ST (a new position) saw initial weakness. $SOFI, another name in this category, reported strong Q2 results.

Looking ahead to August, we anticipate continued focus on inflation data and any further signals from central banks. The Q2 earnings season will also conclude for some of our holdings, including $PLTR and $SHOP. We expect the underlying AI infrastructure buildout to continue at pace, and I remain patient and disciplined in our investment approach, confident that our strategic positioning will be rewarded over the long term.
