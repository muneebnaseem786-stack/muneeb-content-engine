$ETH is currently our largest cost-basis crypto position, representing 12.56% of our portfolio, and it is in a drawdown of -8.6% on cost. I appreciate that a drawdown, even a modest one like this, can feel uncomfortable, but the underlying thesis for $ETH remains intact and has seen significant validation and catalysts in recent weeks.

August was a strong month for $ETH. After consolidating below $1,950 for much of mid-August, Ethereum surged by as much as 23% in 24 hours around August 19-20, breaking above $2,300. This rally was fueled by institutional inflows into spot Ethereum ETFs, with the week ending August 28 seeing a record $824.42 million in net inflows. Regulatory clarity from the SEC and CFTC classifying Ether as a digital commodity earlier this year has opened doors for institutional adoption, and products like the 21Shares Ethereum Staking ETF are now evolving to offer staking yield exposure. $ETH ended August above $2,500, showing stronger relative momentum than $BTC heading into September.

Beyond price action, the network itself continues to evolve rapidly. The upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade, slated for Q4 2026, is a significant step towards boosting Layer 1 throughput and efficiency. This upgrade is targeting a substantial increase in the gas limit to around 200 million and will introduce parallel execution and enshrined proposer-builder separation (ePBS) to enhance censorship resistance and scalability. These are fundamental improvements that reinforce $ETH's position as the leading programmable financial settlement layer. The ongoing testing on testnets like Platåberget is preparing the ecosystem for these changes.

Our thesis for crypto, and specifically $ETH, is rooted in its role as a foundational compute primitive that consumes the same energy and semiconductor components as AI. The network's continuous upgrades, combined with increasing institutional acceptance and demand, reinforce our long-term conviction. I remain comfortable holding our current $ETH allocation, expecting these structural tailwinds to continue to close the gap on our cost basis over time.

We will continue to monitor the Glamsterdam upgrade progress and any shifts in institutional adoption.
