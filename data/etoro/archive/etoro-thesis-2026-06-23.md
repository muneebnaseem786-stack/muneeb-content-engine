## Draft Tuesday Post

Hi Everyone,

One of the most frequent questions in investing, especially during volatile periods, is "when is a thesis broken?". For me, it is a critical distinction that underpins how I manage our portfolio. I firmly believe that price action alone is rarely a reason to sell a position. Seeing a stock in drawdown can be frustrating, but that discomfort does not automatically mean the underlying investment case has deteriorated.

My framework for a broken thesis is strictly tied to a material, sustained change in the company's fundamentals or the competitive landscape that permanently impairs its long-term earnings power. For instance, if a company’s core product adoption stalls significantly, or if its key growth metrics begin to decelerate materially, that could be a red flag. Similarly, if a long-term optionality, such as a major technological breakthrough or a new market expansion, faces persistent regulatory blockages or multi-year delays that were not anticipated, the thesis might need re-evaluation.

We hold our positions based on a multi-year view. For our AI software holdings, for example, a broken thesis would involve a clear reversal in customer count growth or signs that the company is struggling to convert proof-of-concept projects into scaled production deployments. For our electric vehicle exposure, it would be a prolonged plateau in key operational metrics, like fleet expansion, or unanticipated regulatory hurdles that significantly impede its strategic roadmap. These are fundamental shifts, not daily price swings.

This approach means that when a position is in drawdown, I first look for any changes in the underlying business. If the company continues to execute on its stated strategy, grow its customer base, and expand its addressable market, then the thesis remains intact, regardless of temporary price weakness. I am comfortable holding through volatility as long as the fundamental story is playing out.

I will continue to share how this framework guides our portfolio decisions as we navigate market cycles together.
