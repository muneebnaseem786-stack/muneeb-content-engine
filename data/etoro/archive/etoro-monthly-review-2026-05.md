# eToro Monthly Review ,  May 2026

## Month's Market Data
- $SPX500: +4.84% (from 7,230.12 on May 1 to 7,580.06 on May 29), closed at a record high.
- $NDX: The Nasdaq 100 also showed strong performance, with AI-related technology shares having an outsized impact on market-cap-weighted indices. It closed at 30,333.18 on May 29.
- $BTC: approximately -3.7% to -6.0% (from ~$76,550-$78,400 on May 1 to ~$73,700 on May 30/31)
- $ETH: approximately -12.7% (from ~$2,290 on May 1 to ~$2,000 on May 30)
- Other major moves: AI-related technology shares had an outsized impact on market-cap-weighted indices, with Dell Technologies surging over 30% after strong AI-driven earnings, providing a positive read-through for the AI compute cluster. Treasury yields reached multi-year highs.

## Key Macro Events
- **Geopolitics:** The ongoing Iran war and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz continued to impact global oil supplies and prices. President Donald Trump's summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Beijing in mid-May led to an agreement to establish separate boards of trade and investment.
- **AI Policy/Trends:** May saw the global generative AI market projected to reach $121.10 billion in 2026, with hyperscalers' annual capital expenditures anticipated to hit $650 billion. There was a clear trend towards agentic AI systems and deeper enterprise integration, driving demand for compute, chips, and infrastructure. The EU AI Act continued to take shape with provisional agreements on material changes and draft guidance on high-risk systems.
- **US Economy:** US markets were closed on May 25 for Memorial Day. The April jobs report, released on May 8, showed nonfarm payrolls up by 115,000 and the unemployment rate holding steady at 4.3%. The Federal Reserve's commentary in May indicated a hawkish shift in policy, and Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the new Federal Reserve Chairman on May 29.
- **China Economy:** China's factory activity slowed in May, with official and private Purchasing Managers' Indexes (PMI) indicating a moderation in expansion. While domestic demand remained sluggish, high-end manufacturing and exports, particularly in technology and AI, continued to hold strong, alongside significant demand for advanced semiconductor imports.
- **Energy Market:** Brent crude oil prices averaged around $106 per barrel in May due to supply tightness from the Strait of Hormuz closure. U.S. crude oil production forecasts for 2026 were raised.

## Held Names That Reported / Moved Materially
- **$AMD (Advanced Micro Devices):** Reported a strong Q1, with revenue of $10.3 billion and data center revenue up 57% year-on-year. The company provided a robust Q2 guide, validating our AI compute thesis. Further reports highlighted the ongoing production ramp of its EPYC "Venice" processors and a $10 billion investment in Taiwan.
- **$TSM (Taiwan Semiconductor):** Approved an additional $20 billion for its Arizona facility and raised its full-year revenue guidance above 30% growth. The company announced a 15% price increase for its advanced 3nm manufacturing process in the latter half of 2026, with potential further hikes in 2027, driven by strong AI demand.
- **$GOOG (Alphabet):** Faced new regulatory scrutiny as the European Union reportedly plans a "high triple-digit million euro amount" fine for Digital Markets Act breaches. However, the company also reported 750 million monthly active users for Gemini and guided for significant capital expenditure in 2026.
- **$PLTR (Palantir):** Delivered a blowout Q1, with revenue up 85% year-on-year and US commercial revenue increasing by 133%. The company raised its FY26 guidance, reinforcing our thesis.
- **$BABA (Alibaba):** Reported Q4 FY26 results with net profit up 106% year-on-year. Its Cloud Intelligence segment grew 38% year-on-year, and the company provided strong AI ARR guidance. Alibaba unveiled its new Zhenwu M890 AI chip, positioning for full-scale AI commercialization.
- **$NVDA (NVIDIA):** Reported a blowout Q1 FY27, with revenue of $81.6 billion surpassing consensus and data center revenue up 92% year-on-year. The company provided strong Q2 guidance, raised its dividend, and authorized an $80 billion buyback. NVIDIA also confirmed its next-generation Blackwell architecture will be available in the second half of 2026, with major hyperscale customers planning adoption.
- **$SIVE (Sivers Semiconductors):** Released its Q1 2026 interim report, showing a 22% year-on-year decrease in net sales due to government shutdown impacts, but a significant 77% year-to-date growth in its opportunity pipeline to $799 million, with plans for a potential US dual listing.
- **$AMZN (Amazon):** Began selling its AI shopping technology to other retailers and committed to investing over $33 billion in cloud and AI infrastructure in Southeast Asia.
- **$HOOD (Robinhood):** Launched "Agentic Trading" and an "Agentic Credit Card," expanding its offerings into AI-driven finance.
- **$TSLA (Tesla):** The unsupervised Robotaxi fleet continued its expansion, increasing more than fourfold during April and May. Estonia's transport authority cleared Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) system for public roads, with a rollout expected soon.
- **$NFLX (Netflix):** Reportedly held early talks with potential partners to integrate live sports onto its platform, signaling a potential strategic shift.
- **$ONDS (Ondas):** Secured over $30 million in new orders in May, bringing its Q2-to-date orders to over $110 million across its defense, security, and autonomous technology platforms.

## Cluster Behaviour
- **AI compute (~49% of portfolio):** This cluster experienced a very strong month, driven by robust earnings and significant product roadmap advancements from core holdings like $AMD, $TSM, and $NVDA. Hyperscalers such as $GOOG and $AMZN demonstrated accelerating AI infrastructure investments and new monetization strategies, while $PLTR delivered exceptional growth in its commercial segment. The overall narrative of an expanding AI capex cycle and increasing demand for advanced compute remains firmly intact.
- **Crypto (~32%):** The direct crypto holdings, $BTC and $ETH, faced headwinds during May, experiencing negative ETF flows and price declines. Our crypto rails component, $HOOD, however, showed promising innovation with the launch of new AI-driven financial tools. Crypto miners like $IREN continued to benefit from their pivot towards AI cloud services.
- **China (BABA, ~5%):** Our China exposure through $BABA demonstrated resilience, with strong execution in its AI initiatives, including new chip development and cloud revenue growth, hinting at potential for a re-rating despite broader concerns about the Chinese economy's pace of recovery and sluggish domestic demand.
- **Big tech non-AI-primary (~5%):** This cluster saw mixed but generally positive developments. $TSLA's FSD expansion into new geographies reinforced its optionality thesis, and $NFLX explored strategic diversification into live sports content.
- **Tail bets / thematic (~3%):** This segment also had notable activity. We initiated a new position in $SIVE (Sivers Semiconductors) based on its long-term potential in AI optical interconnects and mmWave technology. Additionally, $ONDS, a holding in defense tech, secured significant new orders, validating its growth trajectory.
