# eToro Monthly Review ,  June 2026

## Month's Market Data
- $SPX500: The S&P 500 closed June 2026 down 1.05%, marking a volatile month. It saw an intraweek high of 7,577.92 on June 15 and a low of 7,237.85 on June 9.
- $NDX: The Nasdaq Composite experienced significant pressure throughout June, largely due to a tech sell-off, and posted a net negative return for the month.
- $BTC: Bitcoin closed June 2026 down approximately 18.5%, making it its worst single month of the year. It traded near $58,813 at the start of July, down about 50% from its October 2025 all-time high of $126,272. Key support levels to watch are around $58,000, with resistance near $62,000.
- $ETH: Ethereum closed June 2026 at $1,572.86, down from $2,004.34 in May, marking a decline of over 21%. It slipped below the critical $1,600 level, reaching its lowest levels of 2026, with key resistance at $1,800-$1,900.
- Other major moves: The cryptocurrency market cap slipped to $2.12 trillion, its lowest in close to two years, by the end of June.

## Key Macro Events
- **Jobs Report:** On June 5, the U.S. Labor Department reported a stronger-than-expected May jobs report with 172,000 jobs added and unemployment at 4.3%, fueling concerns about a hawkish Federal Reserve.
- **Inflation Data:** May CPI data, released in mid-June, showed headline inflation accelerating to 4.2% year-on-year, driven by energy prices. The US May Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) report was also higher than expected, contributing to broader inflation concerns.
- **Federal Reserve Stance:** The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting in mid-June maintained expectations for a "higher-for-longer" interest rate environment, with projections for potential year-end rate hikes.
- **Geopolitical Developments:** Initial optimism surrounding a potential US-Iran peace agreement in mid-June briefly eased global risk sentiment and contributed to a market rally, but this sentiment was short-lived.
- **China Policy:** China's new "Ethnic Unity Law" took effect on July 1, 2026, intensifying scrutiny of overseas investments and raising concerns about potential transnational repression and its impact on foreign investment.

## Held Names That Reported / Moved Materially
- **$AMD (Advanced Micro Devices):** Received multiple analyst upgrades from UBS, Citi, and Bank of America, citing strong demand for its MI300 series and EPYC processors, and announced a £2 billion investment in UK AI.
- **$TSM (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing):** Announced plans to increase 3nm chip manufacturing prices by 15% and is shifting capacity towards advanced nodes, reflecting strong AI chip demand. May revenue was up 30.1% year-on-year, and full-year guidance remained above 30% growth.
- **$GOOG (Alphabet):** Announced a $30 billion deal with SpaceX for AI compute capacity, including 110,000 $NVDA GPUs, and expanded its partnership with SAP on generative AI. It also entered a strategic partnership with a major financial services firm for generative AI solutions and is set to join the Dow Jones Industrial Average on June 29.
- **$PLTR (Palantir):** Signed new multi-year, multi-million dollar deals with Google Cloud, McCarthy Building Companies, GNP Seguros, and Kirkland & Ellis, significantly expanding its commercial presence. A partnership with Zeta Global to rearchitect its Data Cloud on Foundry was also announced.
- **$BABA (Alibaba):** Faced a significant headwind as the Pentagon labeled it as aiding the Chinese military, prompting Alibaba to file a lawsuit to challenge the designation.
- **$AMZN (Amazon):** Announced over $30 billion in new cloud infrastructure investments in Illinois and India to support growing AI demand. AWS also confirmed strong AI pricing power and partnered with Salesforce to enhance generative AI capabilities for enterprise customers.
- **$IREN (Iris Energy):** Secured $3.65 billion in financing and a $9.7 billion Microsoft AI Cloud Services contract, along with a $1.6 billion Dell Blackwell GPU order, demonstrating strong execution on its AI cloud pivot.
- **$TSLA (Tesla):** Faced an analyst downgrade due to increasing EV competition and perceived Robotaxi deployment delays. A regulatory probe into a fatal crash involving autonomous driving systems also emerged.
- **$HOOD (Robinhood):** Announced a 10% workforce reduction to streamline operations. It also launched an institutional trading platform and priced a $2 billion convertible notes offering.
- **$COIN (Coinbase):** Launched the first Fannie Mae-eligible mortgage backed by cryptocurrency and pre-IPO perpetual futures for SpaceX. It also launched "Coinbase for Agents" to link crypto accounts to AI agents.
- **$CIFR (Cipher Digital):** Priced an $810 million senior secured note offering to fund a new AI data center pre-leased to Amazon Web Services.
- **$ZETA (Zeta Global):** Formed a strategic partnership with Palantir Technologies to build unified AI marketing infrastructure, targeting over $100 million in annual revenue.
- **$GLXY (Galaxy Digital):** Confirmed building a massive AI data center in Texas, repurposing its Bitcoin mining facility for AI workloads, driving a significant stock rally.
- **$NFLX (Netflix):** Saw some analyst downgrades but also garnered positive commentary on subscriber growth from new content and ad-supported tier monetization.
- **$TAO (Bittensor):** Rallied on increased interest in decentralized AI, received a Grayscale Research endorsement as the premier decentralized AI network, and outlined an 18-month roadmap to full decentralization.
- **$EOSE (Eos Energy):** Signed a major international supply agreement for long-duration storage in Europe and began commercial production on its second battery line.

Hi Everyone,

June 2026 closed as a dynamic and, for some segments of the market, a challenging month. The $SPX500 ended down 1.05%, reflecting broader market volatility driven by persistent inflation concerns and a hawkish Federal Reserve. The tech-heavy $NDX experienced even greater pressure, posting a net negative return for the month as tech stocks faced significant sell-offs, particularly in the latter half of June. Macroeconomic data, including a stronger-than-expected jobs report on June 5, accelerating CPI figures, and higher PCE data, consistently pointed to sustained inflationary pressures, leading the FOMC to reinforce its "higher-for-longer" interest rate stance. This backdrop led to a general risk-off sentiment that weighed on speculative assets.

Our **AI compute cluster**, which forms the largest part of our portfolio, demonstrated resilience and continued strategic progress despite the broader market choppiness. We saw massive capital allocation to AI infrastructure from hyperscalers: $GOOG committed $30 billion to SpaceX for AI compute capacity and expanded its generative AI partnership with SAP, while $AMZN announced over $30 billion in new cloud infrastructure investments in Illinois and India. Our semiconductor holdings, $AMD and $TSM, received multiple analyst upgrades and confirmed pricing power for their advanced AI chips, reflecting robust demand. Notably, several of our crypto-related holdings, including $CIFR, $GLXY, and $IREN, continued their strategic pivot into AI data centers and compute services, securing significant financing and contracts that underscore the convergence of these two megatrends.

However, June was a brutal month for our **crypto-related holdings**. Both $BTC and $ETH experienced severe downward pressure, with Bitcoin seeing its worst monthly performance of the year, falling over 18% to trade near $58,813 by month-end. This decline was fueled by record ETF outflows, a strong US dollar, and the general risk-off macro environment. Ethereum also significantly underperformed, dropping over 21% and facing further headwinds from postponed upgrades and a 20% staff reduction at the Ethereum Foundation. Despite the price action, our long-term thesis on crypto as a fundamental compute demand play remains intact, and we continue to hold our positions, viewing current volatility as part of the asset class's maturation. In brighter news for the cluster, $COIN continued to innovate, launching a Fannie Mae-eligible crypto mortgage and "Coinbase for Agents" to link crypto to AI, while $TAO gained institutional endorsement for its decentralized AI network.

Our **China-related holding**, $BABA, faced fresh geopolitical scrutiny as the Pentagon labeled it as aiding the Chinese military. Alibaba has proactively filed a lawsuit to challenge this designation, a move we are closely monitoring for its potential impact on our China re-rating thesis. Elsewhere in the portfolio, $TSLA saw a mixed month, with an analyst downgrade and a regulatory probe into an autonomous driving fatality, although its long-term optionality thesis remains valid for us despite near-term auto delivery headwinds. Our tail bets and thematic plays delivered important operational wins: $EOSE secured new international supply agreements and launched production at its second battery line, while $ZETA's partnership with $PLTR for AI marketing was a significant catalyst.

On positioning, we opened a starter position in $SIVE at the very end of May, representing a new thematic bet on AI optical interconnects and mmWave technology. We also added to our $TSLA position in May, bringing it to its intended conviction weight, and we currently have no further adds planned for the stock. Our existing $PLTR limit order in the $128-$130 range remained unfilled in May, so that position size is unchanged.

Looking ahead to July, market focus will shift to Q2 2026 earnings, which begin for some of our holdings in mid-July. We also anticipate continued commentary from Federal Reserve officials and further inflation data, particularly the crucial June Nonfarm Payrolls Report, which will be released on July 2. We are positioned for a patient approach through this period of macroeconomic uncertainty, confident in the long-term compounding of our core AI compute and crypto-related theses.
