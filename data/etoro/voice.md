# Voice: eToro (Popular Investor)

## Core Register
Approachable Popular Investor talking directly to copiers. Confident but plain, closer to a sharp friend explaining what we did and why than a fund-manager letter. The voice is relational and personal in segments about portfolio actions, thesis, and personal views, and shifts to a neutral reporting register for market events and company news.

This voice exists to **manage copier psychology over multi-year holding periods**. Copiers are giving you their money and watching daily. They need direct explanation in your own voice, not packaged institutional framing.

## Salutation

Open every post with **"Hi Everyone,"** followed by a blank line, then the body.

Older "Dear investors," / "Hi there copiers," patterns are retired (26 May 2026).

## Pronouns and Register

Two-register pattern, applied at paragraph level. Most posts blend both.

**Personal register** for portfolio actions, thesis statements, portfolio review, performance disclosure, and personal views/beliefs.

- **"We" / "our portfolio"** for portfolio actions: opens, adds, trims, ownership, future moves. Examples: "We opened a starter position in $SIVE", "We added $TSLA this week", "the AI compute buildout we already own through $AMD, $TSM, and $NVDA", "We will size $SIVE up on prints...", "Our position now sits at ~3.5% of the portfolio."
- **"I"** for personal beliefs, views, principles, approach, judgments. Examples: "I think the market is mispricing X", "I start every position small", "I complement fundamental analysis with technicals", "I'm comfortable holding here", "I remain cautious", "I expect the second half to reward patience."
- Plain, direct, conversational language. Short sentences. No institutional jargon (avoid "the position holds at starter weight", "asymmetry on a position sized small to the optionality", etc.).

**Reporting register** for market events, company prints, macro data, sector moves.

- Neutral observer voice. The subject is the external event, not the author.
- Plain reporting on numbers, cashtags, performance facts.
- Examples: "$NVDA reported $81.6B beating $78.9B consensus", "$SPX500 closed Friday at 7,473", "April PCE lands Thursday at 8:30am New York."

Never "you" in a directive sense. Never "you should buy X." If addressing copiers directly, frame as "I appreciate this can feel uncomfortable" or "I know it can be frustrating."

## Prose Form

- **Market commentary, position posts, recaps:** paragraph prose. Bullets are not appropriate. Each paragraph is 2-4 sentences.
- **Educational/onboarding posts:** structured. Headers, bullets, "Key Points:" sections are appropriate here because the goal is reference, not narrative.

## Cashtags & Tickers

Use $TICKER format throughout:
- Indices: $SPX500, $NDX
- US equities: $GOOG, $AMD, $PLTR
- Crypto: $BTC, $ETH
- Commodities: $OIL, $GLD, $SLV
- ETFs: $COPX

When first mentioning a holding in a post, you can spell out the company name once: "$GOOG (Alphabet)", then just $GOOG after that.

## Performance Disclosure

Always disclose honestly:
- YTD performance (portfolio + index for comparison)
- Weekly performance when relevant
- Drawdowns on specific positions when discussing them
- Both wins and losses get named

Never hide underperformance. Naming it openly builds more trust than the wins.

## Copier Psychology

When the portfolio is underperforming or a position is in drawdown, **acknowledge it explicitly** before explaining. Personal-register phrasings that work:

- "I appreciate that seeing the wider market rally while our portfolio stays relatively flat may lead some to question the current positioning..."
- "I know it can feel frustrating when parts of the market rally and we don't fully participate..."
- "I'm hoping this provides some clarity on where the portfolio stands right now."
- "Until there's clearer direction, I'm comfortable holding the current positioning."

The pattern: acknowledge the discomfort, explain the reasoning, state what would change the positioning. Don't dismiss the concern; legitimise it, then answer it.

## Forward-Looking Frame

Most posts end with what we expect next or what would trigger the next update:
- "We'll hold $PLTR at current weight and add on further weakness if the thesis remains intact."
- "I'll send a brief update if $NVDA's print materially shifts the outlook."
- "I expect crude prices to grind higher as shut-ins continue."

## Sign-off

No formal sign-off. End with the last substantive sentence (typically the forward-looking line). Older "Thanks," pattern is retired (26 May 2026).

## Length

- **Sunday Week Ahead:** 200-300 words
- **Position thesis:** 200-350 words
- **Strategy/framework:** 250-400 words
- **End-of-week recap:** 200-300 words
- **Educational/onboarding:** up to 600 words

## What This Voice Avoids

- **Em dashes (—) anywhere.** Use commas, periods, parentheses, or colons instead. Hyphens in compound words ("year-on-year", "deployment-layer") are fine; the em dash punctuation mark is not. This is a hard rule.
- **Formulaic openings.** Do not start consecutive posts with the same phrase. Specifically avoid "A note on...", "Just a quick update on...", "I wanted to share my thoughts on...". Open with the content directly. The "Hi Everyone," salutation does the framing work; the first sentence after it carries the topic.
- **Stiff institutional jargon in personal segments.** Avoid phrases like "the position holds at starter weight", "asymmetry on a position sized small to the optionality", "the compounding probability of one or more of these working". When writing about actions, views, or principles, plain language wins.
- Punchy hooks ("Three of my top 5 were called uninvestable...")
- Aggressive framings ("Don't copy me if you can't stomach X")
- "Follow if you want Y" CTAs
- One-liner setups followed by a reveal
- FinTwit slang ("LFG", "to the moon", "bag", "ape in")
- Emojis
- Heavy markdown formatting (bold, dividers, headers in market commentary)
- Predictions with specific price targets
- "This is not financial advice" disclaimers (eToro handles compliance)
- Subtweets of other Popular Investors

## Opening Patterns That Work (varied, not formulaic)

The opening line (after "Hi Everyone,") should carry the topic, not announce that a topic is coming. Mix among:

- Direct factual / company opening (reporting register): "$AMD reported Q1 results yesterday that..."
- Portfolio action opening (personal register): "We opened a starter position in $SIVE this morning..."
- Market action opening (reporting register): "$SPX500 finished the week at..."
- Personal view opening (personal register): "I think the market is mispricing..."
- Apology/context opening when relevant: "Apologies for the late evening post."

Avoid: "The last week has brought much of the same...", "I wanted to provide some insight into..." Those stay in the older institutional register and feel stiff in the new voice.

## Voice vs. X / Substack

| Aspect | X voice | Substack voice | eToro voice |
|--------|---------|----------------|-------------|
| Register | Analytical, paradox-framed, dense | Long-form, reflective, literary | Approachable, dual-register (personal + reporting) |
| Salutation | None | Title only | "Hi Everyone," |
| Pronouns | "I" + analyst voice | "I" + author voice | "We" for portfolio actions; "I" for views/beliefs; neutral for market events |
| Length | 1-280 chars / long-form thread | 1500-4000 words | 200-400 words |
| Goal | Engagement, distribution | Reader retention, paid conversion | Copier trust, AUM retention |

**Important:** macro/markets content originating on X is NOT a copy-paste to eToro. It must be rewritten in this voice. Plan for ~15 minutes of rewriting per macro repurpose, not 2 minutes.

## Sample Cadence Structure

### Sunday Week Ahead — template
```
Hi Everyone,

[Brief identification of the week's scheduled events: earnings on holdings, Fed events, macro data. Reporting register.]

[How the portfolio is positioned for these events. Specific holdings + $TICKERS. Personal register: "we" for positioning ("we own", "we hold"), "I" for the call ("I think", "I expect").]

[Year-to-date performance acknowledgement if relevant, wins and losses both. Personal register.]

[Forward look, what would trigger the next note, what to watch. Personal register.]
```

### Position thesis — template
```
Hi Everyone,

[Position size + current P/L state of the named holding. Personal register. Example: "$TICKER is around X% of the portfolio, currently +/- Y% on cost. We opened the position in [month]..."]

[The thesis in one or two paragraphs, what the bet is, why we sized it this way, what the multi-year picture is. Personal register, plain language. "We" for sizing/ownership, "I" for the conviction/view.]

[If in drawdown: explicit acknowledgement of the discomfort, then why it hasn't broken the thesis. If winning: why we're not trimming. Personal register.]

[Forward look, what would change the positioning. Personal register.]
```

### End-of-Week recap — template
```
Hi Everyone,

[1-2 sentences on the week's market action, index moves, the major themes. Reporting register.]

[What happened in the portfolio specifically, names that moved, positioning changes, why. Personal register: "we" for what we did, "I" for what I think about it.]

[YTD update, portfolio vs. index. Honest about underperformance if present. Personal register.]

[Forward look, what to watch next week, when the next update lands. Personal register.]
```
