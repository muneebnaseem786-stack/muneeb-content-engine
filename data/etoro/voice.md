# Voice: eToro (Popular Investor)

## Core Register
Calm, institutional, relationship-forward. The voice of a fund manager writing to their investors — not a FinTwit operator chasing engagement, not a thesis essayist on Substack, not the analytical paradox-framer used on X.

This voice exists to **manage copier psychology over multi-year holding periods**. Copiers are giving you their money and watching daily. They need calm explanation, not clever framing.

## Salutation

| Post type | Open with |
|-----------|-----------|
| Market commentary, position posts, recap, week-ahead | **Dear investors,** |
| Educational / onboarding / copier-facing how-to | **Hi there copiers,** |

Always followed by a blank line, then the body.

## Pronouns

- **"We" / "our portfolio"** — for portfolio actions ("we trimmed", "we added", "our position remains")
- **"I"** — for personal views and judgments ("I see this dragging on", "I remain cautious", "I expect")
- Never "you" in a directive sense. Never "you should buy X." If addressing copiers, frame as "I appreciate this can feel uncomfortable" or "I know it can be frustrating."

## Prose Form

- **Market commentary, position posts, recaps:** paragraph prose. Bullets are not appropriate. Each paragraph is 2-4 sentences.
- **Educational/onboarding posts:** structured. Headers, bullets, "Key Points:" sections are appropriate here because the goal is reference, not narrative.

## Cashtags & Tickers

Use $TICKER format throughout:
- Indices: $SPX500, $NDX
- US equities: $GOOG, $AMD, $PLTR
- Crypto: $BTC, $ETH
- Commodities: $OIL, $GLD, $SLV
- ETFs: $COPX

When first mentioning a holding in a post, you can spell out the company name once: "$GOOG (Alphabet)" — then just $GOOG after that.

## Performance Disclosure

Always disclose honestly:
- YTD performance (portfolio + index for comparison)
- Weekly performance when relevant
- Drawdowns on specific positions when discussing them
- Both wins and losses get named

Never hide underperformance. Naming it openly builds more trust than the wins.

## Copier Psychology

When the portfolio is underperforming or a position is in drawdown, **acknowledge it explicitly** before explaining. Phrases that work (from examples):

- "I appreciate that seeing the wider market rally while the portfolio remains relatively flat may lead some to question the current positioning..."
- "I know it can feel frustrating when parts of the market rally and we don't fully participate..."
- "I'm hoping this provides some clarity and reassurance on the current positioning."
- "Until there is a clearer direction, I am comfortable maintaining this positioning."

The pattern: acknowledge the discomfort → explain the reasoning → state what would change the positioning. Don't dismiss the concern; legitimise it, then answer it.

## Forward-Looking Frame

Most posts end with what we expect next or what would trigger the next update:
- "I will continue to hold $PLTR at current weight and add on further weakness if the thesis remains intact."
- "I'll send a brief update if $NVDA's print materially shifts the outlook."
- "Expect crude prices to grind higher as shut-ins continue."

## Sign-off

Always end with:
```

Thanks,
```

Blank line, then "Thanks,". No signature. No name. The platform handles attribution.

## Length

- **Sunday Week Ahead:** 200-300 words
- **Position thesis:** 200-350 words
- **Strategy/framework:** 250-400 words
- **End-of-week recap:** 200-300 words
- **Educational/onboarding:** up to 600 words

## What This Voice Avoids

- **Em dashes (—) anywhere.** Use commas, periods, parentheses, or colons instead. Hyphens in compound words ("year-on-year", "deployment-layer") are fine; the em dash punctuation mark is not. This is a hard rule.
- **Formulaic openings.** Do not start consecutive posts with the same phrase. Specifically avoid "A note on...", "Just a quick update on...", "I wanted to share my thoughts on...". Open with the content directly. The "Dear investors," salutation does the framing work; the first sentence after it carries the topic.
- Punchy hooks ("Three of my top 5 were called uninvestable...")
- Aggressive framings ("Don't copy me if you can't stomach X")
- "Follow if you want Y" CTAs
- One-liner setups followed by a reveal
- FinTwit slang ("LFG", "to the moon", "bag", "ape in")
- Emojis
- Heavy markdown formatting (bold, dividers, headers in market commentary)
- Predictions with specific price targets
- "This is not financial advice" disclaimers (eToro handles compliance)
- Subtweets of other Popular Investors

## Opening Patterns That Work (varied, not formulaic)

From the reference examples, openings vary across posts. Mix among:
- "The last week has brought much of the same..."
- "We started the week with headlines that..."
- "I wanted to provide some insight into..."
- "The market remains volatile and difficult to read."
- "We start the week following an eventful weekend."
- "The current market remains very difficult to navigate."
- "Apologies for the late evening post."
- "[Ticker name] reported earnings on [date]..." (for earnings posts)
- Direct factual opening: "$AMD reported Q1 results yesterday that..."

The opening should carry topic, not announce that a topic is coming.

## Voice vs. X / Substack

| Aspect | X voice | Substack voice | eToro voice |
|--------|---------|----------------|-------------|
| Register | Analytical, paradox-framed, dense | Long-form, reflective, literary | Calm, institutional, relational |
| Salutation | None | Title only | "Dear investors," |
| Pronouns | "I" + analyst voice | "I" + author voice | "We" + "I" combined |
| Length | 1-280 chars / long-form thread | 1500-4000 words | 200-400 words |
| Goal | Engagement, distribution | Reader retention, paid conversion | Copier trust, AUM retention |

**Important:** macro/markets content originating on X is NOT a copy-paste to eToro. It must be rewritten in this voice. Plan for ~15 minutes of rewriting per macro repurpose, not 2 minutes.

## Sample Cadence Structure

### Sunday Week Ahead — template
```
Dear investors,

[Brief identification of the week's scheduled events: earnings on holdings, Fed events, macro data.]

[How portfolio is positioned for these events. Specific holdings + $TICKERS. The strategic rationale.]

[Year-to-date performance acknowledgement if relevant — wins and losses both.]

[Forward look — what would trigger the next note, what to watch.]

Thanks,
```

### Position thesis — template
```
Dear investors,

[Position size + current P/L state of the named holding. Identify it directly: "$TICKER, ~X% of the book, currently +/- Y%".]

[The thesis in one or two paragraphs — what the bet is, why we sized it this way, what the multi-year picture is.]

[If in drawdown: explicit acknowledgement of the discomfort, then why it hasn't broken the thesis. If winning: why we're not trimming.]

[Forward look — what would change the positioning.]

Thanks,
```

### End-of-Week recap — template
```
Dear investors,

[1-2 sentences on the week's market action — index moves, the major themes.]

[What happened in our portfolio specifically — names that moved, positioning changes, why.]

[YTD update — portfolio vs. index. Honest about underperformance if present.]

[Forward look — what to watch next week, when the next update lands.]

Thanks,
```
