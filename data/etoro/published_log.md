## 2026-05-26 (Tuesday Strategy/Framework, "Sell in May" pushback)

> Dear investors,
>
> Every May, the same financial commentators reanimate the "Sell in May and go away" refrain. The data over the past decade has been almost uniformly against them. With the single exception of 2022, a genuine bear market driven by aggressive rate hikes and inflation, stepping aside in May has meant watching the market grind higher into year-end and paying subscription fees for the privilege of missing it. The narrative does not survive contact with the numbers, yet it returns every spring on schedule.
>
> The 2026 numbers extend that pattern. $SPX500 is up roughly 8.58% year-to-date, well ahead of the +4.63% average midterm-year close since 1950. The structural picture on the multi-year $SPY chart is clean: a sequence of higher highs through every major drawdown of the last decade, including 2018, the 2020 pandemic crash, and the 2022 bear. The technology leg from mid-2024 to today is up roughly +67%. Stood against the late-1990s analogue that ran +200% before the dotcom break, the current advance is strong but measured. There is no speculative blow-off in the data.
>
> The pattern across the last six years has been consistent. Single-month drawdowns in $QQQ tied to a macro fear (the COVID crash, the 2023 banking stress, the 2025 tariff escalation, April's Strait of Hormuz tensions) have repeatedly been entry points, not exits. The three completed cycles returned an average of roughly +60% over the 8-to-24 months that followed. April's pullback is tracking the same script, with $QQQ already back near $722 and pushing fresh highs.
>
> This is why I remain bullish on the market through the summer and why the right response to the next pullback is allocation, not exit. Markets climb walls of worry, and history shows they climb furthest for investors who do not step off midway through strong years. I expect the second half of 2026 to reward patience over caution, and the portfolio is positioned for that outcome.
>
> Thanks,

## 2026-05-26 (Tuesday Thesis, $PLTR Pillar 2 — commercial TAM unlock)

> Dear investors,
>
> The second pillar of my long-term $PLTR conviction is the unlock between government and commercial. The US government is one of the hardest customers a software company can win, particularly in defence and intelligence. The security clearances, the procurement cycles, the operational standards, the data sensitivity, and the willingness to depend on a single vendor for mission-critical workloads all create a barrier that almost no enterprise software company has cleared.
>
> Palantir cleared it years ago, and that credential now carries directly into the private sector. CIOs at large industrials, banks, healthcare systems, and energy companies are not signing nine-figure AI deployments based on demo videos. They want to see the workload running in an environment where the cost of failure is higher than theirs, and $PLTR is the rare vendor that can point to exactly that reference. The selling cycle into commercial compresses meaningfully once the government track record is in place.
>
> The commercial addressable market is also structurally much larger than the public-sector pie that originally built the company. US federal IT spend is bounded by appropriations; private sector AI infrastructure spend is bounded only by ROI. The Q1 numbers in early May made the unlock visible. US commercial revenue grew +133% year-on-year to $595M, US commercial customer count reached 615 (+42% year-on-year), and management raised the full-year US commercial guide to at least $3.224B and +120% growth. 206 deals closed at $1M+ in the quarter, 72 at $5M+, and 47 at $10M+. This is not a pipeline narrative; this is bookings.
>
> The position remains around 6.5% of the book at -16% on our cost basis. I am comfortable letting commercial operating performance close the gap over the next two to three years. The third pillar (allied governments outside the US) lands in next Tuesday's note.

> Thanks,
